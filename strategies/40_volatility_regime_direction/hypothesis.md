# Strategy 40: Hypothesis -- Volatility Persistence → Directional Distribution

## 1. Context & Evolution
The systematic progression of Strategies 35–39 established the following boundaries:
1. **Local Candlestick Patterns (35)**: Zero incremental alpha.
2. **Unconditional Directional Long Timing (36)**: Bleeds friction; active timing is strictly dominated by passive Buy-and-Hold.
3. **Pre-Market Overnight Inventory (37)**: Gap continuation is exactly 50.8% (random coin flip).
4. **Opening Auction Behavior (38)**: First 15 minutes of RTH cannot separate trend days from chop days (+0.011 efficiency spread).
5. **Multi-Day Volatility Compression (39)**: Refuted the classical "coiled spring" myth. Instead, proved **volatility persistence**: low-volatility days precede continued low volatility (0.85x ATR), while high-volatility days precede continued high volatility (1.21x ATR).

Strategy 39 successfully delivered our first robust answer to **WHEN**.

## 2. The Core Scientific Question for Strategy 40
We now confront the missing piece:

> **When NQ enters a high-volatility regime, does that regime also change the conditional distribution of direction?**

We strictly decouple the question of **volatility scale** from **directional polarity**:
- We know that previous high volatility predicts future high volatility ($Range \approx 1.21\times$ baseline).
- Does that expanded energy have a directional preference?
  1. Does it exhibit **momentum** (continuation of the prior high-volatility session's direction)?
  2. Does it exhibit **mean reversion** (exhaustion/reversal after extreme displacement)?
  3. Or does it expand **symmetrically** (both upside and downside variance expand equally, leaving direction as a 50/50 coin flip)?

## 3. The Scientific Value of the Null
If direction remains approximately 50/50 regardless of volatility state, this is NOT a failure of research; it is a profound scientific discovery:
> **The market gives us a predictable "WHEN" (volatility clustering), but NOT a "WHICH WAY" (unconditional directional edge).**

This distinction establishes whether subsequent research should focus on directional predictors conditional on high volatility, or on non-directional volatility monetization (e.g. regime-based trade throttling).
